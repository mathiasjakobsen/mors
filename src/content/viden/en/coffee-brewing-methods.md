All coffee brewing comes down to the same thing: hot (or cold) water extracting flavour from ground coffee. What sets the methods apart is **how long** the water is in contact with the coffee, **how fine** the coffee is ground, and whether the water **flows through** or **steeps**.

## Espresso

Water is forced through very finely ground coffee under high pressure in 25–30 seconds. The result is a small, concentrated cup with body and crema – and the base of a cappuccino, flat white or latte.

Behind the bar we dial in the espresso every morning and through the day: if it tastes sour, we grind finer; if it tastes bitter, coarser. See [our espresso recipe](/en/brewguides/espresso-brew-guide).

## Pour over

Water is poured in circles over coffee in a filter – usually a **V60**, Kalita or Chemex – and drains through on its own. Pour over gives a clear, clean cup where the bean's own character – fruit, florals, chocolate – comes through. It is the method that best shows what speciality coffee can do. Try it at home with [our V60 brew guide](/en/brewguides/v60-pour-over-brew-guide) – exactly the recipe we use.

## Filter coffee (batch brew)

The same principle as pour over, just in a larger batch on a machine. Good filter coffee is nothing like office coffee: with fresh beans and the right ratio it is one of the best ways to taste a bean. This is [how we brew filter coffee](/en/brewguides/filter-coffee-brew-guide).

## French press

Coarsely ground coffee steeps in the water for about four minutes before you press the plunger down. There is no paper filter, so the oils end up in the cup and the coffee is full and round. Forgiving and easy at home – follow [our French press guide](/en/brewguides/french-press-brew-guide).

## AeroPress

Part steeping, part pressing: the coffee steeps briefly in the chamber and is then pressed through a small filter. Quick, easy to clean and good for a single cup – also when travelling. Try [our AeroPress recipe](/en/brewguides/aeropress-brew-guide).

## Cold brew

Coffee steeped in cold water for many hours. Ours steeps for 18 hours. The cold gives a smooth, sweet cup with low acidity – perfect over ice in summer.

## Which one should I choose?

- **Want to taste the bean clearly?** Pour over.
- **Want it easy and full-bodied?** French press.
- **One quick cup?** AeroPress.
- **Milk drinks?** Espresso – or drop by the coffee bar and we will make it.

You will find all the recipes under [brew guides](/en/brewguides).

Whatever the method, three things matter: fresh beans, grind just before brewing, and use [good water](/en/knowledge/water-for-coffee).
